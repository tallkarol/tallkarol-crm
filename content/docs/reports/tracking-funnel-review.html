01

The answer, up front

With the access you granted this week I can now read all four systems — the Tag Manager container, Analytics, the Google Ads account, and the CRM — and reconcile them against each other for the first time. The short version: the site-level tracking built in July is genuinely good, and every connection downstream of it is broken.

The site records the right events with the right detail, and the CRM captures the ad click behind every enquiry. But Analytics is still watching for a conversion event that stopped existing in July; Google Ads imports that same dead event as its only lead conversion, so the ad account has spent years optimising with zero examples of success; and consent settings mean Analytics sees roughly one in twenty of the ad clicks you pay for. Nothing here needs rebuilding. It needs about a dozen configuration changes, listed in section 06 in order.

A correction to the Ads review

Two days ago, working from Analytics alone, I wrote that the competitors campaign's volume collapsed in August — 28 visits, down from 212. The ad account says the opposite: clicks rose every month (418 in June, 533 in July, 714 in August). The campaign never slowed down. What collapsed was measurement — the new site asks for cookie consent and most visitors never answer, so Analytics stopped seeing them. The campaign is healthier than I reported, and the tracking is worse.

Where the funnel is strong

Once someone starts the form, it works: 46 starts became 16 recorded enquiries — and the enquiry mix is right, mostly custom manufacturing. The paid economics, visible for the first time, are startling: at $438.67 since the cutover, a tracked conversion costs about $55 and an enquiry about $88. The Ads review estimated $400–1,000 per enquiry from benchmarks. Reality is five to ten times cheaper.

Where it leaks

Above the form. Roughly 600 measured sessions produced ~147 real call-to-action clicks, which produced 46 form starts. The homepage takes most of the traffic and hands almost none of it to the form — the same landing-page problem as the Ads review, now with numbers on every step. And below the form, the pipeline is still: no enquiry has moved a stage, so nothing downstream can be judged yet.

One channel worth watching: AI assistants

ChatGPT sent 9 measured sessions in 90 days and 2 of them became CRM enquiries — the best session-to-enquiry rate of any channel, on a tiny sample. This traffic only grows. It costs nothing and asks only that page URLs stay stable and the site keep answering the questions it already answers well.

03

Tag Manager: what is in the container

First direct look at container GTM-5B83HMG (live version, published 17 July). The good news first: the core is clean. Seven GA4 event tags fire from the site's own dataLayer — cta_click, form_start, form_step, form_submit, generate_lead, catalog_download, plus single-page-app page views — each carrying useful detail (which form, which enquiry type, which step, which button). This is better instrumentation than most sites this size ever get.

Four problems around that core

the big one

There is no Google Ads conversion tag

A Conversion Linker tag dutifully stores the ad-click cookie on every visit — and then nothing ever reports a conversion back to Google Ads. The plumbing for the return signal was never connected. Fixing this (via the Analytics import in section 05) is the highest-value change in this report.

stale config

The Linker lists the wrong websites

Its cross-domain list names mineralife-frontend.vercel.app and mineralife.wpenginepowered.com — a staging address and the old WordPress host. Neither is the live site. Harmless day to day, but it documents that nobody has looked in here since the migration.

dead weight

Leftover tags from three eras

A paused Universal Analytics tag (product retired 2023), a paused Hotjar tag, a Facebook pixel that waits for a job-fair page from a past hiring push, and a LinkedIn Insight tag still loading on every page for a LinkedIn campaign the Ads review already showed produces nothing. None of it is measuring anything; all of it is weight and clutter.

design trade-off

Tags load late, and only after consent

The site loads Tag Manager after the page finishes rendering (good for the speed scores) and keeps analytics off until the visitor accepts cookies (good for compliance). The cost is the 4.5% visibility figure from section 01. The site partly compensates by sending enquiry events server-side, which is why the CRM out-counts Analytics — the right architecture, with two wiring faults noted in section 04.

04

Analytics: conversions & settings

Both “conversions” are ghosts

The property has exactly two key events configured, both from July 2023: form_submission — the old WordPress form's event, which the new site never sends — and purchase, an e-commerce event with no meaning on a business-to-business site. Result: every channel, campaign, and page in Analytics shows zero conversions, forever, while the real events (generate_lead, 23 recorded; catalog_download) sit in the event list unmarked. This is the settings change the Ads review asked for, now with the exact event names to use.

Data is deleted after two months

Event-level retention is set to the default 2 months instead of the free maximum of 14. Detailed June data is already gone; every month that passes, history two months back is erased. One dropdown fixes it — for data collected from that day forward, which is why it is urgent.

The useful detail is collected, then ignored

The site sends eleven descriptive fields — enquiry type, form step, which button, where on the page — but none is registered as a custom dimension, so none can be used in reports. The only two registered dimensions are Universal Analytics leftovers (event_category, event_label) that nothing sends any more.

Enquiries are double-counted

When a visitor has accepted cookies, an enquiry fires generate_lead twice — once from the browser, once from the server backstop. Hence 23 recorded against 16 real enquiries. The server copy also carries no session context, so 6 of the 23 float attached to no page and no channel. Small fixes to the server call solve both.

Housekeeping

The dev machine (localhost:3000, 23 sessions) and syndicatedsearch.goog bot traffic (67 sessions, 111 fake button clicks) pollute every rate. The web stream's registered URL is mycustommanufacturing.com — a domain that isn't yours (note the spelling). The property's timezone is Los Angeles; the business runs on Mountain time. Each is minutes to fix.

What this means

Both B2B campaigns run “maximise conversions” bidding. The only lead conversion they could optimise toward is wired to an event that never fires. Google has been told to maximise something it has never once observed — it has been bidding blind the entire time, which goes a long way to explaining the search-term quality below. The five enquiries happened despite the setup, not because of it.

The Regional campaign has not shown a single ad in 90 days — its bids lose over 90% of the auctions it enters. It is a zombie: on, budgeted, and producing nothing. The competitors campaign also loses 27% of its possible impressions to its $10 budget cap — it would spend more if allowed, relevant to the raise-the-budget question.

Where the $900 actually went

One ad group, broad-match competitor keywords. The keyword nutra labs took $470 — half the spend, two-thirds of the clicks — and broad match stretched it to searches like “custom gummy vitamin manufacturer” (194 clicks, $58, for a product the site does not offer), “supplement startup”, and “pharmaceutical manufacturing”. 72% of clicks were mobile at $0.27 apiece — cheap for a reason. The five enquiries almost certainly came from the genuine competitor searches buried in this; without a conversion signal, Google cannot tell those from the junk.

The economics, honestly stated

Since the cutover: $438.67 → 5 enquiries + 3 catalog downloads, all click-verified in the CRM. That is ~$88 per enquiry for the highest-value enquiry type the business has, against an industry benchmark of $400–1,000. Even if half the spend is junk clicks, the half that works is exceptional. The case for spending more is strong — after the conversion wiring exists, so the extra budget learns from day one. Five enquiries is still a small sample; treat the $88 as a first reading, not a law.

06

What to fix, in order

Everything below is configuration, not construction. First group this week, second when convenient, third is strategy. I can do all of the first two groups; two items need your Google Ads sign-in to approve.

this week — turns the lights on
  • Analytics: mark generate_lead and catalog_download as key events; delete form_submission and purchase. Fifteen minutes, and every report gains a conversions column.
  • Google Ads: import those two key events as conversions; make generate_lead the primary goal for both B2B campaigns (campaign-level goal settings, so the Zemvelo retail goals stop steering B2B bidding and vice versa). Demote “Page view” from primary regardless — it is noise in anyone's account.
  • Analytics: retention 2 → 14 months. One dropdown; only affects data from that day forward, so every day of delay is a day lost.
  • Google Ads: pause the Regional zombie, or rebuild its bids — currently it only reserves budget and attention.
  • Google Ads: add negatives on the competitors campaign (gummy, pharmaceutical, startup, and friends) and tighten nutra labs to phrase match. Half the budget is riding on that one broad keyword.
next — makes the data trustworthy
  • Register the event detail as custom dimensions (enquiry type, form id, step name, CTA id/location) so reports can finally answer “which button, which page, which enquiry type”.
  • Fix the double-counted enquiry: send the server-side event only when the browser copy cannot fire, and attach the session id so server events stop floating unattributed.
  • Filter out syndicatedsearch.goog, and the dev machine via an internal traffic rule — together they fake more button clicks than real paid traffic produces.
  • Correct the Conversion Linker's domain list, remove the LinkedIn / Facebook / UA / Hotjar leftovers, fix the stream URL spelling and the timezone.
then — makes the data valuable
  • Close the loop: the CRM already stores the ad click behind every enquiry. When enquiries start moving through pipeline stages, upload those outcomes back to Google Ads (start manually, monthly) so bidding learns which clicks became business, not just form fills. This is the single biggest long-term lever, and it is blocked today only by the pipeline standing still — all 16 enquiries still sit in “New”.
  • Give paid traffic a landing page with the form on it — the funnel now shows each step of what the homepage detour costs.
  • Revisit budget once conversions flow: the competitors campaign is budget-capped and losing 27% of its impressions; raising it is defensible the day Google can see what a conversion is.
  • Longer term, consider server-side tagging so measurement depends less on the cookie banner. Worth a conversation, not urgent.
