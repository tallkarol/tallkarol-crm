01

What this month actually shows

Version three was a report about a broken instrument. It found that Analytics had been recording roughly one visitor in twelve since 17 July, and nothing at all for three days at the start of September. That was fixed on 4 September. This report is the first in the series written from numbers that are actually measuring the site.

Four things stand out, and none of them is the thing the account was set up to prove.

The fix worked, and held for twenty-four days

September recorded 1,164 visitors against August’s 131 — not growth, but the same traffic finally being counted. Analytics now captures about 58% of what the cookieless counter sees, against roughly 8% before. Nothing has regressed since.

LinkedIn is now half of all traffic, and it is the worst traffic on the site

638 of September’s 1,248 sessions came from LinkedIn paid social, at engagement rates of 17% and 2%. Google’s paid search traffic engages at 60%. LinkedIn produced no enquiries. It is also not in the Google Ads account, so nothing in the previous reports has been watching it.

A month of rebuilt search advertising produced no enquiries at all

$474.54 bought 160 clicks across the two B2B campaigns. The traffic is genuinely relevant and genuinely engaged. It converted nothing. September’s five enquiries came from direct visits, organic search and a trade-show referral — not one from advertising.

Organic search opened a door nobody has walked through

An entire cluster of commercial queries the site did not rank for in August now sits in Google’s top five — twenty-five vitamin-manufacturing terms, one of them at position 1.1 — and every single one of them earned zero clicks. Sections 09 and 10 set out what appeared and what to write to claim it. This is the cheapest opportunity in the report by a wide margin, because the hard part is already done.

Most of this series has been a list of things that were broken. September is the first month where the opposite list is longer, so it is worth setting out plainly.

Not one enquiry came from advertising

160 paid clicks and 108 paid search sessions produced zero enquiries in September, and the 638 LinkedIn sessions produced zero as well. Every enquiry the site received came from somewhere that costs nothing per click: a direct visit, an organic search, a trade-show exhibitor page, and a directory listing.

This also settles a question left open in version three. Google Ads reports zero conversions not because the import is broken — it is correctly configured, enabled and primary — but because there has genuinely been nothing to import. The reporting is honest. The advertising is what is not working.

Two of the four enquiries are worth noticing for what they suggest about where effort pays. The SupplySide exhibitor page and the comanufacturers.com directory are both low-cost listings, and between them they produced as many leads in September as $474 of search advertising and an unknown LinkedIn budget did: two, against zero.

The pattern, and why it is the most important finding in this report

Twenty-five of the site’s best-placed commercial queries — several thousand impressions, many inside the top three — produced zero clicks between them in September. That is not normal. Position three on a commercial term should convert somewhere around one impression in ten.

The explanation is in section 08. Almost all of these impressions land on the homepage, and almost all of them land on the version of the homepage without www — 25,509 impressions at position 5.3, converting 0.14%. One generic page is absorbing dozens of specific queries and satisfying none of them.

Why that is happening, structurally

The site is organised by service model — custom manufacturing, outsourced, toll filling, white label — and by location, with fourteen state and city pages. It has no pages organised by product category. But the demand that has just appeared is almost entirely category-shaped: vitamins, probiotics, sports, herbal, protein. With nothing specific to rank, Google ranks the homepage for all of it, and a visitor searching for a vitamin manufacturer lands on a page about contract manufacturing in general and leaves.

What to write next month

Every recommendation below comes from a query the site already ranks for and earns nothing from. That is the cheapest kind of content to justify: the ranking is proven, the demand is measured, and only the landing page is missing.

Read this before commissioning any of it

A search ranking is evidence that people are looking, not evidence that Mineralife can make the thing. Each item below needs a yes or no on capability before a word is written — publishing a page about probiotic manufacturing the plant cannot do would be worse than publishing nothing. Where the answer is no, the right move is to say so and drop the item, not to hedge the copy.

The six worth doing, in order

1 Vitamin manufacturing — a capability page, not a blog post Evidence: ~2,500 impressions across 25 new queries, positions 1.1–6.8, zero clicks. vitamin manufacturer near me is at position 1.1.
Write: a service page covering forms (liquid, capsule, tablet, softgel, powder), vitamin D3 and B12 specifically since both surface as their own queries, minimum order quantities, and certification. This is the single largest untapped cluster in the report.
2 “How to choose a supplement manufacturer” — an honest evaluation guide Evidence: best supplement manufacturers went from 114 to 1,374 impressions and improved from position 6.9 to 3.8. Related: supplement manufacturers usa 295 impressions at 2.1, custom supplement manufacturer 407 at 6.3.
Write: this query wants a comparison, and a self-serving “why us” page will not hold position 3.8. Write the criteria a brand should actually judge a manufacturer on — certifications, MOQs, lead times, formulation support, what a certificate of analysis should contain. Link the existing compliance posts into it; there are nine of them and they are good. Mineralife wins by being the one that explains the test honestly.
3 Probiotic manufacturing Evidence: five queries, ~1,470 impressions, positions 5.1–7.5, zero clicks — probiotic manufacturers 407, probiotic supplement manufacturers 369, probiotics manufacturers 356, probiotic contract manufacturer 173.
Write: capability page plus one supporting post on the thing buyers actually worry about with probiotics — viability through manufacturing and shelf life, CFU guarantees at expiry rather than at fill. Capability check needed first.
4 Strengthen the turnkey post that already exists Evidence: the turnkey cluster is new (466 impressions) but ranks 9–12.5 — the weakest placement of anything in this report — and the account paid $4.86 for one click on the same phrase.
Write: nothing new. /blog/turnkey-manufacturing-solution exists and is underperforming; it needs expanding into what turnkey actually includes step by step, and internal links from the four service pages. Cheaper than a new page and it lifts a term being paid for.
5 Sports supplement manufacturing Evidence: sports supplement manufacturers 302 impressions at position 3.1, zero clicks.
Write: this is the easiest win on the list because the supporting content is already published — three posts on NSF Certified for Sport, including why pro athletes require it and how banned-substance testing works. A category page that pulls those together and names the certification is close to assembly rather than authorship.
6 Herbal, and protein powder Evidence: herbal supplement manufacturing 279 impressions at position 5.7. plant protein manufacturers 99 at 9.0, and the ads paid $4.99 for a click on wholesale protein powder manufacturers.
Write: one page each if the plant can do them. Smaller clusters than the first three, so these are the ones to drop if the month runs short. Capability check needed.

Two things not to write

avoid Anything about processing equipment nutraceutical powder processing equipment is the site’s biggest impression gain of the month — 454 to 3,014 — and it should be ignored completely. Mineralife manufactures supplements; it does not sell machinery. The position of 13.2 against 2.9 for terms that do match is Google saying the same thing. Chasing this would bring traffic that can never buy.
avoid More compliance and certification FAQs There are already 39 blog posts and the library leans heavily this way — nine on cGMP, NSF and 21 CFR, six on liposomal delivery. They are good posts and they are not the gap. The question-shaped queries in September are tiny, one or two impressions each; the demand that has actually appeared is commercial and category-shaped. Another explainer would be writing into a space that is already full.

One caveat on all of it

None of this content pays off while the homepage listing problem in section 08 persists, because new pages inherit the same fate: ranked, shown, not clicked. The two fixes are the same fix seen from opposite ends — give each query its own page so the generic homepage stops absorbing them, and rewrite the listing text so what does rank earns the click. Do the title and description rewrite first; it takes an afternoon and it tells you whether the click problem is the listing or something else entirely.
