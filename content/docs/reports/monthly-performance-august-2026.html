The month in short

Seven enquiries came through the website in August. Visits fell sharply — but almost all of that fall is advertising that stopped running, not the website losing ground. Search visibility actually improved: the site appeared more often and ranked three places higher on average.

The finding worth your attention is underneath those numbers. The site was shown in Google 32,878 times in August. Almost none of those turned into a visit, and of the clicks Google can attribute to a search term, 88% were people typing your company name. In other words the site is being seen by people looking for supplement manufacturing, and picked by people already looking for Mineralife.

Where the drop came from

July carried 358 visits from paid advertising — 209 from a Google Ads campaign named “B2B – Search – Competitors” and 149 from LinkedIn. In August that was 25 visits from Google Ads and none from LinkedIn.

That accounts for 333 of the 566 lost visits. Most of the rest is knock-on: paid visitors who return directly later show up as direct traffic the following month, and July had 189 direct visits against August's 47.

This needs confirming with you — we can see the advertising in the analytics but we do not run it, and we do not know whether it was paused deliberately, ran out of budget, or stopped for another reason.

Almost every click is someone already looking for you

Google attributes 68 of August's clicks to a specific search term; it withholds the rest to protect rare or personal searches. Of those 68:

60clicks on your name 7 search terms, all variations of “Mineralife”. 219 times shown.
8clicks on everything else 1,108 search terms. 28,964 times shown.

Everything that is not your company name converts at 0.03%. That is the single number this site turns on, and it is not a ranking problem in the usual sense — the pages are being found.

These are the searches where the site is shown most and clicked least. Positions between 8 and 12 are the bottom of page one and the top of page two — seen by people scrolling, chosen by almost nobody.

Enquiries

Seven enquiries from 137 real visits — a 5.1% enquiry rate, which is strong for business-to-business manufacturing.

84 people asked to get in touch. 21 started.

Once someone starts the form, a third finish it — that part is working well. The widest step is between clicking a button and beginning to fill anything in: 63 people took an action that says “I am interested” and then did not begin.

Some of that is normal — a call-to-action click includes people browsing to the contact page to read it. But a quarter of them starting the form is a number worth trying to move.

Recommendation: put the first field of the form on the page the button leads to, rather than asking for a second click. It is a small change and this is the step with the most people standing on it.

06

What we recommend next

1. Close the gap between the button and the form

123 of 145 people who clicked a call-to-action never started the enquiry form. Nothing else on this list is worth as much.

2. Decide what the advertising is doing

Paid traffic dropped from 358 visits to 25 between July and August. Whether that was deliberate changes what this report should be measuring next month.

3. Fix the hiring post's address

Fifteen minutes, and it removes the only genuine technical defect the scan found.

4. Talk about the 28,964

Being shown constantly for “supplement manufacturer” and never clicked is either a titles-and-descriptions problem or a positioning one. It deserves its own session rather than a line in a report.

About these numbers

  • Two different tools, two different counts. Google Search Console recorded 131 clicks from search; Google Analytics recorded 54 visits from unpaid search. The gap is the cookie consent banner — visitors who decline are counted by Google's search tools but not by the site's analytics. Both numbers are correct; they measure different things.
  • Dates are not identical. Search figures run 31 July – 27 August, the window Google reports as final. Visit figures run 1 – 27 August.
  • Search terms are incomplete by design. Google withholds the terms behind roughly half of the clicks to protect rare searches, which is why the brand split is given as a share of the 68 attributed clicks rather than all 131.
  • Automated traffic has been removed. 49 August visits came from Google's search-syndication network and 8 from a developer machine. Between them they produced 74 call-to-action clicks and never started a single form, so leaving them in would have understated the enquiry rate by roughly half. They are excluded from every figure in this report; the raw totals were 203 visits and 158 call-to-action clicks.
