Where things stand

Version one described a system whose plumbing was sound and whose wiring was not. As of tonight the wiring is done too. The site-side work shipped over the weekend; the Analytics side was finished on 1 September; and on 2 September the Google Ads account itself was rebuilt — conversion goals, match types, negatives, networks, landing pages, ad copy, and a second campaign, all verified live against the API after the changes were made.

The whole chain, closed

For the first time since the account was opened: an advertisement is clicked → the click ID is stored server-side before consent → the visitor lands on a page built for the search with the form on it → the enquiry fires generate_lead with the form location attached → Analytics counts it as a key event → and Google Ads counts it as the primary conversion it bids toward. Every link in that chain was verified individually today.

What the rebuild cannot change

Bidding still has zero conversion history to learn from — the signal only began existing tonight. Expect the first weeks of “maximise conversions” on Competitors to behave much as before until real enquiries teach it otherwise, and read September as the first month of a system that can learn, not a month that has already learned.

What the cookieless counter revealedNew

Analytics is close to blind, and it is worse than v1 assumed

Version one put it at roughly one paid click in twenty. Measured against a real denominator, 31 August settled at one visitor in twelve once Analytics finished processing the day — the first pass of this report caught it mid-processing at three sessions — and 1–2 September show nothing at all against 129 real visitors. The zero days may still fill in (31 August tripled overnight), so read them again on 4 September before treating them as final. The direction is unambiguous either way.

Nothing is broken. The container is on the page, all eight Analytics tags are live, and version 13 is serving — verified directly again this morning. The Analytics configuration tag is gated on analytics_storage consent, so a visitor who never touches the banner is never counted. This is the cookie banner doing exactly what it was built to do.

What this changes about reading Analytics

Every session, event and rate drawn from Analytics in this report is a floor rather than a total, and a very low floor. Analytics stays useful for proportions — which page, which form, which step — and is close to useless for volume.

Why the enquiries are still trustworthy

Enquiries are recorded server-side with their advertisement click, independent of consent. The lead records are the truth at the bottom of the funnel and the advertising account is the truth at the top. Analytics only fills in the middle.

The counter also answers the geography question

Of the 237 real visitors between 31 August and 2 September, 125 were in the United States — 53%, against 1.8% of August's paid clicks. The site's audience is American; the advertising was not. Two referrers are worth watching now that Search Partners are off: 45 visitors arrived from syndicatedsearch.goog (Google's partner-site ad network) and 16 from Google's video-ad player. A search-only account should send neither. If they persist past this week they are not coming from these campaigns.

The measured cost of a paid enquiry

August: $307.49 produced two enquiries carrying a click ID and one catalogue download carrying one — the same visitor fetched it twice, which the first pass of this report counted as two. That is $154 per paid enquiry, or $102 per paid outcome if the download counts.

Higher than the ~$88 quoted in v1, which covered a longer window with a better month in it. Against a $400–1,000 category benchmark it remains cheap. Three outcomes is far too small a sample to call a rate — treat it as an order of magnitude, not a figure.

September so far

Through 2 September, account time: Competitors $2.11 and 23 clicks, all on the 1st under the old configuration, then 7 impressions and no clicks on the 2nd; Core Terms $0 — approved and eligible, no impressions yet. No enquiries this month, and no August enquiry has moved stage in the pipeline.

September's numbers will be a before/after seam: two days of the old configuration, then the rebuilt account. Read the month accordingly.

What changed on Competitors2 Sep

Every recommendation from the morning draft was applied the same day, several of them improved on in the applying. Budget and geography deliberately untouched.

Lands on a page built for the searcher

Final URL is now /lp/switch-manufacturers — a better choice than the contract-manufacturing page this report originally suggested, because someone searching a competitor's name is weighing a switch, and that is the page written for exactly that moment.

The ad was rebuilt, not patched

Fifteen new headlines drawn from approved site copy, pinned in three rotating groups of five — intent, differentiator, qualifier — replacing eleven near-duplicates and the pinned competitor-trademark headline. Display path /switch/transfer. Approved and serving. Google rates its ad strength Poor (the Switching Intent ad is Average) — pinning all fifteen headlines into three slots is what drives that rating, and it is a deliberate trade for message control.

A second audience, separated — but barely eligible

A new Switching Intent ad group — eight phrase/exact terms like “switch supplement manufacturer” and “supplement manufacturer not responding” — with its own approved ad. Competitor-name searches and I-want-out searches now report separately. The caveat this re-run found: Google flags seven of the eight terms as rarely served — too little search volume to be eligible until it appears. Only new supplement manufacturer is serving. Treat the ad group as a listening post, not a traffic source.

Waste controls, all live

Broad match paused across the board. 174 negatives attached. Search Partners off. Eight rewritten sitelinks (the old set included one pointing at a 404 and four at redirect chains) — seven approved; Toll Filling is disapproved on both campaigns although its page returns 200, so the reason needs reading in the Ads UI. Five callouts. All re-verified against the API this morning.

One flag carried forward: the headline “FDA Registered Facility” appears nowhere in the site's approved copy. The ad passed Google's review; confirming the claim with Mineralife is section 10's business, not Google's.

11

Decisions still open

1. Overseas brands half-answered

The new callout on Competitors reads “Ships Internationally” — which, if deliberate, answers the question this report has carried since v1. Worth saying out loud so it is a decision rather than a side effect: Competitors stays worldwide and welcomes overseas enquiries; Core Terms is the US-only arm. If that is the position, it is a coherent one — the two strongest pipeline enquiries are Pakistani and Bangladeshi.

2. Tablets and gummies

The 174-term negative list is live on both campaigns. Worth a five-minute read-through to confirm gummy and tablet exclusions match real capability — a negative keyword is invisible once set, and custom gummy vitamin manufacturer was 126 clicks in August.

3. What happens at the first attributable enquiry

Now imminent rather than hypothetical. Agree in advance what it triggers — budget up, hold a month, or reshape keywords — so the decision is made against the plan rather than against the excitement.

4. Private label answered

Ruled out. Recorded so the reasoning is not relitigated: it removes roughly 590 searches a month from reach and takes the white-label ad group below the threshold where its landing page earns its keep.

5. Phone calls from ads new

Competitors carries a call asset, and “Calls from ads” is a primary action at account level — but the campaign's Submit-lead-form-only goal means a phone lead from an ad is not a conversion the campaign can see or bid toward. With 79% of clicks on mobile that is either a real gap or a deliberate choice. Decide which; adding the phone-call category to the campaign goals is a two-minute change if it is the former.
