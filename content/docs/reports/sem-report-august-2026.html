01

Summary

This month's work was a synchronisation exercise: getting Google Tag Manager, Analytics, Ads and Search Console into one connected system that can be measured, reported on and improved. Four tools were running. None could see each other, two could not be read at all, and the one carrying the advertising budget had never been told what a successful outcome looks like.

They are now connected, and the finding is a hopeful one. The tracking built into the new website in July is genuinely good. The site records the right events with the right detail, and every enquiry is stored with the advertisement click that produced it. What is broken is everything downstream of the website — a dozen configuration settings, not a rebuild.

~$88per paid enquiry Against a $400–1,000 category benchmark. Five to ten times cheaper.

4.5%of paid clicks visible 1,028 clicks billed since the site launch; Analytics recorded 45 paid visits.

An important correction — the advertising did not slow down

Read through Analytics alone, paid traffic appeared to collapse in August. The advertising account says the opposite: clicks rose every month — 418 in June, 533 in July, 746 in August — on roughly $300 of spend a month throughout.

What collapsed was measurement. The new site asks for cookie consent and most visitors never answer, so Analytics stopped seeing them. The campaign is healthier than it appeared; the tracking is worse. This is the single clearest illustration of why the systems needed connecting.

The four things that matter most

  1. The advertising has never been able to count a lead. Both business campaigns run “maximise conversions” bidding, and the only lead conversion available to them is wired to an event the new website stopped sending in July. Google has been told to maximise something it has never once observed. The five enquiries happened despite the setup, not because of it.
  2. Half the advertising budget rides on one broad keyword. A single term took $470 of $900 over ninety days, and broad matching stretched it to searches like custom gummy vitamin manufacturer — 194 clicks for a format Mineralife does not make.
  3. Analytics is deleting your history. Event-level retention is set to two months rather than the fourteen available at no cost. June detail is already gone. Every day of delay loses another day permanently.
  4. Organic visibility is high and currently worthless. The site was shown 32,878 times in August and earned 131 clicks. Excluding brand searches, 28,964 impressions produced 8.

The economics, honestly stated

Since the site launch, $441.51 of spend produced five enquiries and three catalogue downloads, all verified by click identifier. That is roughly $88 per enquiry for the highest-value enquiry type the business receives, against a published benchmark of $400–1,000 for business-to-business manufacturing.

Even if half the spend is junk, the half that works is exceptional. The case for spending more is strong — but after the conversion wiring exists, so the additional budget learns from its first day rather than repeating three months of blind bidding. Five enquiries remains a small sample: treat $88 as a first reading, not a law.

One enquiry never reached the tracking sheet — the German enquiry sits in the website's records and in no sales tracking, apparently never seen.

09

The geography question

Almost none of the paid traffic is American, and the enquiries follow it. This determines what next month's budget should do, and it is a commercial question rather than a technical one.

The case for restricting to the United States

Only 3.4% of paid visits were American. The ideal client profile describes established brands shipping 40,000–300,000 units a year, and overseas enquiries carry freight, regulatory and payment complexity that domestic ones do not. One of the five had to be marked dead as a solicitation.

The case against

The strongest enquiry is Pakistani and sits at 50% probability in evaluation. The second strongest is Bangladeshi. Restricted to the United States this campaign would have produced almost nothing — the American audience has not been reached at this budget with these keywords.

Why this cannot be settled by a split test

Proving one audience converts better than another, at the difference worth caring about, requires roughly 390 clicks in each arm — and only to detect a gap as crude as one converting at half the rate of the other. At American click prices that is $20,000–24,000 before anything is learned; a realistic, smaller difference costs into six figures. The current campaign also cannot be split by geography, because its American half would contain almost no traffic.

What is affordable is a comparison rather than a proof. Cost per enquiry firms up quickly — ten enquiries gives a usable figure, twenty a solid one — and whether the enquiries are better is a judgement your sales team can make by looking at them. At this sample size that judgement is more reliable than any statistical test the budget could buy.

Ad group 1 goes first not because it is largest but because nutraceutical contract manufacturer is 720 searches a month at $7.95 — a quarter the price of the term beneath it at similar competition. It is the cheapest genuine demand available, which makes the first month of learning the least expensive it will ever be. gmp certified supplement manufacturer also stands out: 210 searches at the lowest competition of any term tested.

Deliberately excluded

Toll filling by name. Fifteen terms tested, 30 searches a month between them, and all 30 belong to a term that is not supplements. It is the flagship service and the highest-margin division, and there is no paid demand for the words — so it is sold on the contract manufacturing page rather than given a campaign of its own.

Anything geographic. All seven regional terms return no volume, which is exactly why the Regional campaign has never shown an advertisement. The regional pages may still earn their keep organically; there is nothing to bid on.

Landing pages

Four pages, one per ad group, assembled from sections the website already publishes and given an enquiry form on the page itself. This is the single largest identified leak: the homepage took roughly 1,400 visits in ninety days and produced four form starts, because it has no form on it.

Why this pays for itself

Google scores how well a landing page answers the search that reached it, and that score feeds the price of the click. A page built for the term is usually cheaper to advertise than a homepage, so the work can pay back before any improvement in enquiries.

Two rules when building them

Copy assembled only from what the site already publishes, so nothing new needs approval and nothing drifts from what has been signed off. And excluded from the organic index, so they cannot compete with the service pages for the same searches.

12

Decisions we need from you

1. Do we advertise on “private label”?

590 searches a month at $24.30 — the second-largest block in the plan and larger than the entire custom manufacturing group. The terminology does not match how Mineralife describes itself, which is a sound reason to avoid it in organic content, where you cannot rank on a word your copy refuses to use.

Paid is different: the keyword is what the buyer types, while the advertisement and the page still answer in your words. Our recommendation: advertise on it, in its own ad group so it can be judged and switched off cleanly. Excluded, ad group 2 falls from 790 searches a month to 200 and no longer justifies its own landing page.

2. Do you want to manufacture for overseas brands?

Restricting to the United States makes the advertising match the ideal client profile and removes the solicitations — and would also have excluded the two strongest enquiries received this period.

Our recommendation: do not restrict the existing campaign yet. Leave it running, launch the American campaign alongside it on a separate budget, and let two months of cost-per-enquiry figures and your sales team's read on quality answer it with evidence rather than instinct.

3. What is the monthly advertising budget?

The existing campaign loses 27% of its available impressions to its $10 daily cap, so it would spend more if allowed. At $88 an enquiry the arithmetic is attractive — but a third more of what it is currently buying is not obviously worth having until the negative keywords and the conversion signal are in place.

Our recommendation: complete this week's fixes first, then fund the American campaign at a level that produces around ten enquiries in sixty to ninety days. Below ten, the cost figure is too imprecise to decide from.

One thing that needs your team rather than a decision

No enquiry has yet moved beyond the first stage in tracking, so nothing below the form can be judged. Once qualified, quoted and won outcomes are recorded and returned to Google, the advertising optimises toward the kind of enquiry that becomes a purchase order rather than toward whoever fills in a form. With five enquiries a month that takes one short conversation to maintain, and it is the difference between buying traffic and buying customers.

Appendix — tag and analytics configuration

Detail behind sections 02 and 11, from a direct read of the Tag Manager container and the Analytics property.

Tag Manager

The core is clean

Seven event tags fire from the website's own data layer — button clicks, form starts, form steps, form submissions, enquiries, catalogue downloads and single-page navigation — each carrying useful detail about which form, which enquiry type, which step and which button. This is better instrumentation than most sites of this size ever receive.

the big one

No Google Ads conversion tag exists

A linker tag dutifully stores the advertisement click identifier on every visit — and then nothing ever reports a conversion back. The plumbing for the return signal was never connected. This is the highest-value change in the report.

stale

The linker names the wrong websites

Its cross-domain list contains a staging address and the retired WordPress host. Neither is the live site. Harmless day to day, but it records that nobody has looked inside the container since the migration.

dead weight

Leftover tags from three eras

A paused Universal Analytics tag for a product Google retired in 2023, a paused Hotjar tag, a Facebook pixel waiting for a job-fair page from a past hiring push, and a LinkedIn tag still loading on every page. None measures anything; all of it is weight.

design trade-off

Tags load late, and only after consent

The site loads Tag Manager after the page finishes rendering, which protects the speed scores, and keeps analytics off until cookies are accepted, which protects compliance. The cost is that Analytics sees about one in twenty paid clicks. The site partly compensates by sending enquiries server-side — the right architecture, with the wiring faults noted above.

How these numbers were measured

  • Advertising figures — the Google Ads API directly. Spend, clicks, impression share, search terms, devices and geography are the account's own numbers.
  • Enquiries — the website's own records, where the Google click identifier is stored on the server when a form is submitted. More complete than Analytics, which loses visitors who decline the cookie banner.
  • Enquiry stages — your lead tracking sheets, unedited.
  • Organic search — Google Search Console for the domain property. Google withholds the terms behind roughly half of all clicks, which is why the brand split is expressed as a share of attributed clicks rather than of all clicks.
  • Visits and funnel steps — Google Analytics, with automated traffic excluded. Treat these as a floor rather than a total, for the consent reasons above.
  • Keyword volumes and click prices — third-party United States estimates, used only for planning the campaign that does not yet exist. Market estimates, not figures from your account.
  • Category benchmarks — published business-to-business industrial figures, used only where this account has no data of its own, and labelled where used.
  • Known data-quality caveats. Test records were created and removed while systems were being configured, and enquiries were double-counted in Analytics, so July's event counts do not reconcile; August is the first clean baseline. Analytics recorded far fewer paid visits than the advertising account billed clicks — explained in the appendix, and being corrected.
