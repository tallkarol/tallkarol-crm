What this is

Every page in your sitemap is checked against Google's own index each month: whether Google has it, whether it can fetch it, whether it agrees with the address you gave it, and whether anything is blocking it. Findings carry forward month to month, so a problem that appears in August and is still there in October shows as one item seen three times — not three new problems. An item disappears from the list only when Google stops reporting it, which is the same thing as it being fixed.

One of these has a broken address

The hiring post's URL contains an encoded emoji — %f0%9f%93%a2 is a 📢 that survived the move from WordPress. Google reports the page as completely unknown to it, which for a URL that has been in the sitemap since July points at the address rather than the content.

Recommendation: republish at a clean URL and redirect the old one. Roughly fifteen minutes.

The other three are a judgement call

Two regional pages and one blog post. Google knows the site well — it has crawled 59 other pages in the last six weeks — so passing these over is a signal about how it rates them, not an accident.

Recommendation: either strengthen them with internal links from pages Google already visits often, or drop them from the sitemap. Leaving them listed and thin does neither.

Reading this honestly

Impressions are up 11% and average position improved by three places, so the site is being shown more often and higher. Clicks fell anyway. That combination means the site is surfacing on searches it isn't the right answer for — more visibility on the wrong queries.

It is a pattern, not a blip. Across the sixteen months now archived, impressions have grown roughly fourfold while clicks have stayed flat near 130 a month. Fixing indexing will not change it; the queries themselves are the next piece of work, and that sits outside this maintenance package.
