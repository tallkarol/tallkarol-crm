01

Why this is one plan, not two

Two questions were open: build a campaign that matches what the business sells now, and find out whether American leads cost less or convert better. They have the same answer, because the only way to get meaningful American traffic is to build that campaign — the existing one has drawn 13 US visits in three months, so there is nothing there to test.

What exists today

One campaign, B2B – Search – Competitors, bidding on rival brand names with no geographic restriction. It has produced five enquiries at an 11% conversion rate — genuinely good — but 96.6% of its traffic is non-US, and nothing in the account catches somebody searching for the service itself.

What this adds

A US-targeted campaign on the terms buyers actually type, landing on pages built for those terms. It fills the positioning gap and, by running alongside the incumbent on its own budget, answers the geography question as a by-product.

What is deliberately not here

Toll filling by name. Fifteen terms, 30 searches a month between them, and all 30 belong to toll blending services which is not supplements. It is the flagship service and the highest-margin division and there is no paid demand for the words. It gets sold on the contract manufacturing page instead.

Anything geographic. All seven regional terms return zero — Colorado, Colorado Springs, Denver, and the rest. The 14 /serving pages may still earn their keep organically; there is nothing to bid on.

Both of these feel like they should work. Neither has demand behind it, and finding that out cost $0.09 rather than a month of budget.

03

Landing pages

Four pages, one per ad group, assembled from sections the site already publishes and given a form. Cheap to build, and worth more than the geography question itself — every campaign benefits.

Worth doing regardless

Every paid visit currently lands on the homepage, which has no form on it, and the widest step in the funnel is between clicking a button and starting to fill anything in. A page that answers the query it was clicked from, with the first field visible, addresses that directly. Google also scores landing-page relevance and that feeds the cost per click, so a purpose-built page is usually cheaper to advertise than a homepage.

Two rules when building them

Keep the copy page-sourced. Assemble from sentences already published — the MOQs, capsule sizes, fill range, the NSF 455-2 line. Nothing new to approve and nothing that can drift from what is signed off.

Mark them noindex, follow. They deliberately overlap the service pages; left indexable they would compete with those pages for the same searches, fixing one channel by damaging another.

Build the contract manufacturing page first, and point the old campaign at it too

Otherwise the comparison breaks before it starts. If the new campaign gets purpose-built pages and the competitors campaign stays on the homepage, a better US result tells you nothing — it could be geography, keywords, or simply that one arm had a landing page and the other did not.

Both arms on real landing pages keeps the comparison about audience. The cost is that the competitors campaign's own before-and-after breaks on that date, so record it.

04

How it gets judged

Not as an A/B test — that is not affordable

To prove one audience converts better needs about 390 clicks in each arm, and only to detect a gap as crude as 11% against 5.5% — roughly $20,000–24,000 at US click prices. A realistic gap of 11% against 9% costs $181,000–219,000. Real differences are small and small differences are the expensive ones to prove.

You did not ask for proof. You asked what an American lead costs, which needs far less data — and whether they are better, which is Ethan looking at ten enquiries and saying which he would rather have. At this sample size his judgement beats any significance test you could buy.

05

Order of work, and the one open decision

1

Get Ads account access

Read access for reporting, or a 90-day export of campaign, keyword and search-terms reports. Everything else can proceed without it; nothing can be judged without it.

2

Build the contract manufacturing page, point both campaigns at it

One page, page-sourced copy, form above the fold, noindex. This alone should move the incumbent campaign's numbers.

3

Launch ad group 1 only

nutraceutical contract manufacturer at $7.95, US-only, own budget. Cheapest real demand, so the first month of learning is the least expensive it will ever be.

4

Add the other three pages and ad groups

White label, certifications, custom — each switched on with its page, not before it.

5

Qualify with Ethan monthly, and re-run the review

First checkpoint 30 September, real cost-per-lead answer by late November.

The open decision: do we buy “private label”?

590 searches a month at $24.30 — the second-largest block in the whole plan and larger than the entire custom manufacturing ad group. The keyword sheet marks it DO NOT TARGET on terminology grounds, which is right for organic, where you cannot rank a page on a word your copy refuses to use.

Paid is different: the keyword is what the buyer types, and the ad and the page still answer in your words. My call: buy it, phrase match, in its own ad group so it can be judged and switched off cleanly. If it is out, ad group 2 drops from 790 searches a month to 200 and stops being worth its own landing page.
