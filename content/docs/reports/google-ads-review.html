01

The answer, up front

Google Ads has produced 5 enquiries at $88.25 each. Every one arrived after 21 July, every one is a custom-manufacturing enquiry, and every one came from B2B – Search – Competitors (campaign 20436468427), which spent $441.26 in that window.

$88 a lead is well under the $250–500 that published benchmarks put on a qualified B2B manufacturing lead, so on cost alone this looks cheap. Two things complicate it: the campaign is buying clicks at 43 cents, which is not what serious buyers cost, and Google has recorded zero conversions for it because no working conversion action exists.

Correcting an earlier number in this report

I previously put this campaign's conversion rate at 11.1%. That divided 5 enquiries by 45 Analytics sessions. The account actually paid for 1,026 clicks in the same window, so the real click-to-enquiry rate is 0.49%. Analytics only ever saw about 4% of the traffic that was paid for.

That gap is itself a finding — a thousand paid clicks that never became a measurable visit is either people leaving before the page loads or clicks that were never really people. It does not change the cost per lead, which comes from spend and is $88.25 either way.

Why the window starts on 17 July

The code that records a Google click ID against an enquiry shipped with the new site on 17 July. Before that date an ad click could produce an enquiry and leave no trace, which is why June looks like 325 paid visits and zero leads — the leads may well have happened and simply were not recorded. Everything in this report is measured from the cutover forward, on the 45 visits we can actually account for.

One campaign, one landing page, one enquiry type. The traffic is reaching the right kind of buyer — nobody arrived asking about a 250-unit catalog order. And the campaign kept producing after its volume collapsed: August sent 28 visits and still returned two enquiries.

All five still read as New, and that is a tooling gap

The consequence is real but it is not a reason to wait: until stage history flows back automatically, a good ad click and a bad one look identical to Google. Analytics saw 2 of these. The CRM has 5.

Google Analytics recorded two paid enquiries in this period; the CRM recorded five. The CRM is right — it stores the click ID server-side when the form is submitted, while Analytics loses anyone who declines the cookie banner. Judging this campaign on the Analytics number alone would have undercounted it by 60%, and it is the number most reporting would have used.

Four things wrong with the current setup

costing you leads

Every ad points at the homepage

518 of 566 paid visits landed on /, which has no enquiry form on it — eleven buttons that lead somewhere else, but nothing to fill in. Across the whole site, 145 people clicked a call-to-action in August and only 22 began a form. Paid traffic is paying for that gap at full price.

wasting spend

77% of the clicks are mobile, at a fifth of the price

792 mobile clicks for $163.53 — 21 cents each — against 217 desktop clicks for $272.27, at $1.25. A contract-manufacturing buyer researches at a desk, and desktop is where the real cost per click sits because that is where the real competition is. The campaign is buying four cheap mobile clicks for every expensive desktop one.

the biggest single problem

The campaign is not bidding on competitors

It is called “Competitors” and the search terms it actually paid for are almost all generic, and several are for things Mineralife does not make:

This is what a broad-matched campaign with no negative keyword list does, and it explains both the 43-cent click and the 0.49% conversion rate: the campaign is buying a lot of very cheap attention from people asking for something else.

stale

Ads still point at pre-migration addresses

Paid traffic arrives at /request-a-quote/, /quality/ and /about/ — pages from the old WordPress site. They all redirect correctly, so nothing is broken, but every click takes an extra hop and the ad copy is pointing at a site that no longer exists in that shape.

still broken in Ads

Google has no way to count a Mineralife lead

The account has sixteen conversion actions. Every enabled one belongs to Zemvelo's shop — purchase, add to cart, begin checkout. The only lead-shaped action, Form Submission (All Web Site Data), is HIDDEN and is a Universal Analytics goal, a system Google retired. The Mineralife GA4 action that does exist is a purchase event, also hidden.

So the campaign's 0 conversions is not a reporting quirk — there is genuinely nothing for it to count. Marking generate_lead as a key event in Analytics on 30 August was the necessary first step; it still has to be imported into Ads as a conversion action and set as the primary goal before any bidding can respond to it.

One more source worth naming

70 visits came from syndicatedsearch.goog, an automated Google search-syndication service. It produced 116 call-to-action clicks and zero form starts — more button clicks than any real channel and no human behaviour behind any of them. It is not costing you money, but it is inflating the click figures that a conversion rate gets divided by. It should be filtered out of reporting.

04

What I recommend

Keep the competitors campaign — it is the only thing in the account producing enquiries, and at 11% it is producing them well. Fix what surrounds it, retire LinkedIn, and put the unspent budget into a campaign that matches what the business actually sells now.

keep and fix

1. B2B – Search – Competitors

Five enquiries at $88 each, all custom manufacturing. It is the only thing in the account producing B2B leads and it is worth keeping. But it is not working the way the name suggests, and the fixes are now specific:

  • Add negative keywords first. gummy, gummies, pharmaceutical, soft gel, softgel, industry, jobs, salary. Gummies alone is 130 clicks of the last six weeks.
  • Import the conversion action so Ads can count an enquiry at all.
  • Cut mobile bids. 77% of clicks, and the desktop click costs six times as much because that is where the real buyers are.
  • Send it to a page with a form and update the stale final URLs.

Expected effect: the same traffic producing more enquiries, before a penny more is spent. That is the correct order.

Reviewed 30 September. This campaign is the one on trial. The question it has to answer is not “did it produce enquiries” — it did — but whether those enquiries are worth having, which needs the qualification loop in section 06.

keep as the control

2. LinkedIn paid social — runs until Google Ads has proved itself

The numbers are poor: 303 visits, 8% engagement, an average visit of 6.4 seconds, zero recorded enquiries. My instinct was to stop it. Your call is to keep it until Google Ads is proven, and that is the more careful position — turning off the only other source of paid traffic at the same moment as rebuilding the search side would leave nothing to compare against, and any change in enquiries would be unattributable.

So it stays as the control, and the 30 September review judges both together. One caveat worth holding: LinkedIn's zero is measured the same way Google's five is, so it is a fair comparison — but it is a comparison between a channel we are about to improve and one we are leaving alone, which is not a level test of the channels themselves.

build

3. A campaign for what the business is now

You are right that nothing in the account matches the new positioning. And there is budget sitting idle to do it with: B2B – Search – Regional has a $5 daily budget and has served zero impressions — which the keyword data explains exactly, because all seven regional terms have no search volume at all. That campaign should be retired and its budget moved. Live pricing puts the real demand here:

measure

4. Before the budget goes up, not after

  • Mark generate_lead as the key event — done 30 August. Still to do: import it into Google Ads as a conversion action and make it the primary goal, which is the half that actually changes bidding.
  • Filter out the syndication traffic and the developer machine, so rates are real
  • Qualify the five open leads with Ethan and record the outcome — without that, a second enquiry from the same campaign teaches us nothing
  • Send those outcomes back to Google, so bidding learns which clicks became business rather than which became form fills

Which event is the enquiry: generate_lead

Of the nine events in the property, that is the one that means “somebody asked us to get in touch”. form_submit is its mechanical twin and should not also be marked — marking both counts every enquiry twice, and Google would then bid toward a number that is double the truth. cta_click, form_start and form_step are funnel steps, useful to watch and wrong to optimise toward.

Why July's numbers do not tie out. generate_lead fired 16 times in July against 10 form_submits and 6 CRM enquiries. That is test leads being created and deleted while the CRM was being set up, not a tracking fault. August is already tighter — 8, 8 and 10 — and will keep tightening as the CRM settles. The practical rule: treat August as the first usable baseline and ignore July, and revisit these reconciliations in 30 to 60 days rather than chasing them now.

On raising the budget — and the question that is now answered

I said earlier that we could not tell whether this campaign was limited by its budget or by how few people search those terms. Now we can: it is the budget. The campaign holds a 10.0% impression share and loses a further 27.2% of available impressions to running out of money. There is roughly a third more of this traffic available at today's prices.

Whether you want a third more of it is the harder question, because of what section 03 shows it is buying. My call: fix the negatives and the conversion action first, then raise it. Raising the budget on the campaign as it stands would buy 27% more gummy searches.

Expect the new US campaign to look worse per click and better per lead. This one buys clicks at $0.43 and converts 0.49% of them. Head terms cost $8–38 and convert at a published 3–6.5% — a cost per enquiry in the hundreds rather than $88, but from people actually describing the service. Cheap leads and good leads are not the same purchase, and the September comparison is really between those two things.

The qualification loop, and why it is the important part

The CRM is an internal tool and nobody is working out of it yet, so lead status lives with Ethan. The plan for September is that you and he go through the enquiries together, decide which were real, and record that judgement — qualified, quoted, or not a fit.

That sounds like admin and it is actually the thing that makes the advertising improve. Right now Google is told a form was filled in and nothing more, so it optimises toward whoever fills in forms. Once outcomes go back, it optimises toward the kind of person who turns into a quote. That is the difference between buying traffic and buying customers, and it needs roughly one conversation a month to keep running.

Five enquiries is a small enough number to do by hand this month. It is worth building the habit while it still is.

Sources, and a correction

An earlier version of this report said we had no Google Ads access

That was wrong. The CRM has had a working Google Ads integration all along — a developer token, a client in lib/insights/google.ts, an adsSource adapter, and customer id 6243702566 already stored against the site. Everything in this version comes from that API directly.

Three conclusions changed as a result: the campaign is budget-limited, not demand-limited; the conversion rate is 0.49% of clicks paid for, not 11% of sessions Analytics happened to see; and the cost per lead is a measured $88.25 rather than an estimate. The direction of the recommendations did not change. The reasons behind them did.

  • Enquiries are counted from the CRM, where the Google click ID is stored when the form is submitted. 46 enquiries all-time, 5 carrying a click ID.
  • Visits and engagement come from Google Analytics, property 395780153.
  • Search volumes and costs per click are live third-party estimates pulled on 30 August 2026, not figures from your account.
  • Campaign attribution on enquiries is by Google's automatic tagging. No UTM campaign names are recorded on any lead, so enquiries can be traced to Google Ads and to a campaign id, but not to an ad group or keyword.
