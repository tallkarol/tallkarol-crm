The five things a bank would have caught on day one

resolved

The brand row was a tense problem, and the homepage already fixed it

The pending row asserts a third-party manufacturer that doesn't sell its own consumer brand. As written it is unsourced — that idea appears on 0 of 63 pages and “Zemvelo” appears zero times. The only nearby sentence, /our-story's “Starting from our own brand…”, describes the origin: Mineralife began as a liquid-minerals consumer brand, that brand became Zemvelo, and the Mineralife name now carries the manufacturing arm. Both sentences are true at different times.

You've since approved the present-tense form — an award-winning contract manufacturer for established brands. It turns out the site says that already, as the h1 of the homepage:

“Award-winning supplement manufacturing for established brands”

So the row promotes on page authority after all, once it's rewritten to what the site actually claims — and the award behind “award-winning” is substantiated on three further pages, though not consistently (below). The finding worth keeping is the mechanism: an extractor that matched on topic rather than on the exact sentence would have promoted the /our-story line and sourced the opposite of what the row means. Verbatim text and a first-seen date are what stop that.

absent

“2,500 units” appears nowhere on the site

The positioning doc gives toll filling a 2,500-unit small run. Zero occurrences across 45,386 words. What the site publishes instead, on /services/toll-filling, is a flat MOQ 5,000 units — and /services/custom-manufacturing shows “SMALL RUN (ADDITIONAL FEE IF ACCEPTED) 5,000 units”.

So the internal doc and the public site disagree about the cheapest way into a toll project. That's a sales-facing discrepancy, and it's exactly the class of thing that gets into an ad headline and then into a contract argument.

vocabulary conflict

The possessive rule is wrong, not the site

CLIENT.md is emphatic: always NSF's Certified for Sport®, with the possessive, and never “NSF Certified for Sport” in copy that asserts the certification.

“NSF's Certified for Sport”0 pages
“NSF Certified for Sport”63 pages

The live site uses the non-possessive form universally, including in the site-wide meta description. Under your ruling that the site is approved copy, the pack's VOCABULARY slot is the thing that needs correcting.

precision gap

The site is more specific than the claim

The pending row says “NSF cGMP certified”. The site names the actual standard on 19 pages. /why-choose-mineralife:

“Mineralife operates under NSF 455-2 cGMP Certified standard.”

A bank sourced from pages inherits that precision for free. A claim bank sourced from a positioning doc rounds it off — and “NSF cGMP” is the weaker line to take into a market where the buyer knows what 455-2 is.

conflict

Two pages name the award, and they name it differently

The homepage says “award-winning” and never says which award. Three other pages do — and two of them give it a different title.

/blog/mineralife-named-top-10-contract-manufacturer, 22 Sep 2022 — “named a Top 10 Contract Manufacturer by Manufacturing Outlook”
/certifications, authored — “recognized Mineralife among its Top 10 Contract Packaging Services Companies”

Contract manufacturing and contract packaging are not the same category, and the second is the weaker of the two for a manufacturer's ad. Both are client-approved copy, so both are assertable under your ruling — which is precisely the situation the bank has a conflict state for. Nobody was going to notice this by reading; it needs two sentences put side by side.

Your call: ads use Top 10 Contract Manufacturer, and /certifications stays as written. That is the case the design has to handle properly, because the conflict is now permanent: the site will keep saying both things, so a crawler that re-raises the disagreement every run is noise, and one that quietly drops the losing sentence is lying about what the site says. Neither is acceptable. The resolution is a third thing — a dated decision recorded against the pair, keeping both facts, marking one preferred and the other superseded, so the next crawl recognises a settled conflict instead of discovering a new one.

What this does to part two

Google Ads copy is an assertion in a paid placement to a B2B buyer, which is the most exposed surface in the engagement. With a page-sourced bank, 28 of the 30 specs become writable with a URL behind each — the MOQs, the capsule sizes, the fill range, the certification. Without it, every headline is either vague or a pending row.

03 — the shift

Three objects, two of them new

The current model has one object — a Claim — and one rule about pages, which is that they don't count. Splitting the middle out is what lets the rule stay true and the site still be useful.

new

A Page

A crawled artifact with a fetch date. URL, canonical, title, meta, heading tree, main-content blocks, image list, JSON-LD types. Asserts nothing. It's a photograph of the site on a given day, and its only job is to be diffable against the next one.

data/mineralife/pages/<date>/

new

A Fact

One quotable assertion, verbatim, with the page and block it came from, a first-seen and a last-seen date. Not a summary — the actual sentence. A fact whose sentence disappears on a later crawl doesn't vanish; it goes stale, which is a state you can act on.

data/mineralife/facts.json

exists — gains a source type

A Claim

Unchanged in shape, still the only thing an agent may assert. What changes is that Source gains a machine-checkable form — page:<url>@<date> — alongside the human ones. Certificate-backed and page-backed claims sit in one table and are told apart by their source, not by living in different files.

clients/mineralife/claims.md

Why a Fact isn't just a Claim with a URL

Because the two have different failure modes, and collapsing them hides both. A claim goes wrong when it was never true — the fix is a document. A fact goes wrong when the page changed underneath it — the fix is a re-crawl. Keeping them separate is what buys the decay check: the crawler can say “MIN-C-07 cites a sentence on /services/toll-filling that is no longer there” without anyone having to remember the claim was page-sourced in the first place.

It also keeps the original rule intact where it matters. A fact is still an observation of an artifact. Promoting one to a claim is a decision with a date on it, not a side effect of reading a page.

A page is evidence that the client said it. A certificate is evidence that it's true. For most sentences the first is the bar; for six rows in this pack it isn't.

The six are the certification rows. Your ruling promotes them on page authority, and that's your call — but the bank should still mark them gated:document, so the day a certificate lands the row upgrades rather than being rediscovered. Certification is the one category where a buyer's contract, not Google's ad review, is the thing on the line.

04 — the extractor

Chrome is not a fact

The naive version of this — regex the page text, keep the sentences mentioning a spec — produces something worse than nothing. Here's what it produced on the real crawl.

417raw matches Sentences mentioning a certification, MOQ, size or scope limit.
279unique 138 of the matches were the same sentences seen again.
34pages, one sentence The site-wide meta description, counted 34 times as 34 findings.

What the naive pass thinks it found

“Services ▾ Certifications Who we work with Why Mineralife ▾ Contact Knowledge hub Mineralife is Hiring 📢 June 30, 2022 · Mineralife Nutraceuticals is looking to hire a Soft Gel Manufacturing Expert with experience in food or cGMP dietary supplement manufacturing—21 CFR 111 or 21 CFR 117.”

That is the first “sentence” of /blog/mineralife-is-hiring, verbatim from the crawl: the nav, the page title, a 2022 date and a real 21 CFR reference glued together because the tags between them were stripped without inserting a boundary. It matches on cgmp and on 21 cfr, and it would enter the bank as a compliance fact. Every page produces its own variant.

Four rules that make the output usable

  • Scope to main content. Strip header, nav, footer and the consent banner before extracting. The site is Next.js with consistent landmarks, so this is a selector list, not a heuristic.
  • Dedupe by normalised text, keep every location. One fact, N pages. A sentence on more than about a third of the site is chrome, and is recorded as chrome rather than as a finding.
  • Attribute to the most specific page. A spec appearing on /faq and on twelve /serving pages belongs to /faq; the rest are echoes.
  • Never join across a tag boundary. Insert a separator when flattening, so a heading and the paragraph under it stay two things.

Search for the word and you miss it; search for the thing and you find it

Checking whether the site substantiates “award-winning” by searching for award returns exactly 1 page — the homepage, which is the page making the claim. The site never uses that word for it. Searching for the entity instead — Manufacturing Outlook, Top 10 — returns 3 pages, including a dedicated post and a paragraph on /certifications. Same corpus, same question, opposite answer.

This is why the extractor keys on named entities, figures and units rather than on the claim's own vocabulary: the page that proves a claim is rarely the page that phrases it the way the claim does.

The densest page on the site is 414 words long

/faq is among the shortest pages on the site — shorter than every /serving page and most of the blog — and carries the most fact payload: published MOQs for all three divisions, the full capsule size list, the bottle count range, the fill range, and all four scope limits in one sentence. Weighting extraction by word count would rank it near the bottom. Fact density is the useful signal, and here it's nearly the inverse of length.

Two commands, same discipline as the rest

docs/output-contract.md currently freezes the snapshot and queue shapes. The bank needs one more frozen shape and one more bounded read, so agents get facts the way they get opportunities — capped, cited, and never by opening a file.

insights crawl   --site mineralife [--cadence daily|weekly] [--force] [--dry-run]
                                   # fetch, extract, diff against the last crawl
insights facts   --site mineralife [--kind commercial|capability|certification|scope]
                                   [--state promotable|unsourced|conflict|superseded|stale]
                                   [--origin authored|templated|inherited] [--limit 20]

facts defaults to a hard limit like every other command, because 279 facts is already past the point where dumping them into context is safe.

--cadence is the re-crawl interval, not a scheduler: it sets how old the last crawl has to be before this one does any network work, so the same command is safe to run daily or hourly and only fetches when the interval has elapsed. --force overrides it. The per-site default lives in config/sites/mineralife.json as contentSources.cadence, so switching Mineralife from weekly to daily is a one-word config edit and no code change — and when something does eventually run on a clock, it calls the same command with no new flags.

06 — the asset bank

699 image tags, 53 actual images

You asked for ad assets drawn from the site's own photography. The crawl says that pool is smaller than it looks, and that the binding constraint is compliance rather than resolution.

699<img> tags Across 63 pages — mostly the same logos and icons repeated.

58%alt coverage 408 of 699. Alt text is the only description of what a photo shows.

Every candidate needs a compliance pass, not a crop

COMPLIANCE line 2 in CLIENT.md is a hard line: never name or imply a customer brand, and no photos of labeled finished goods that identify one. A contract manufacturer's photo library is full of exactly that — filled bottles with labels on them. So the asset bank carries a review state per image, and anything showing a labeled product is needs-karol until you say otherwise, however good it looks in a 1.91:1 frame.

58% alt coverage is the selection problem

Nothing in a crawl knows what a photograph depicts except its alt text and the page it sits on. With 42% of tags unlabelled, roughly half the pool can only be described as “an image on /services”. Enough to build the inventory, not enough to pick assets from it — so the bank records the gap rather than guessing, and filling it is a cheap job across 53 files that also improves the site.

07 — sequence

Build order

Four increments. Each lands on its own, and the first two are worth having even if you never build the rest.

1

The crawler and the page store

Sitemap in, one JSON per URL out, main-content scoping and block boundaries done properly. Written under data/<slug>/pages/<date>/ so a second crawl diffs against the first. The config already names the sitemap.

Ships: the site becomes readable by anything, at a bounded token cost.

2

Extraction, dedupe, and the coverage report

Facts out of blocks, chrome separated from findings, and every pending claims.md row tested against the corpus — the table in section 02, generated rather than hand-made. Conflicts and absences are output, not exceptions.

Ships: the 28 / 1 / 1 answer, repeatable, on any client with a pack.

3

Promotion into the claim bank

The page:<url>@<date> source type, the promotion write into claims.md, and insights facts as the bounded read. Promotion stays a reviewed step — the command proposes a diff, you merge it.

Ships: agents can write about this client, with a citation per sentence.

4

The decay check

Re-crawl on the interval set by --cadence, mark facts whose sentence has gone, flag any claim whose page source is stale, and stay quiet about conflicts that already carry a decision — a settled disagreement only re-raises if one of the two sentences actually changes. This is the increment that keeps the bank honest twelve months from now, and the one that makes the whole thing safe to trust.

Ships: a page-sourced claim can go stale instead of going wrong quietly.

The asset bank falls out of step 1 as a by-product — the crawler already collects every img — but its review pass is a human job and belongs with part two, where the assets are actually needed.
