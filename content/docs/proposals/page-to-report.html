02 — the argument

One chain, eight links, two broken

The three parts were separate documents because they were written on separate days. They are one argument, and it is easier to judge as one: a sentence on a page should be able to travel all the way to a line in a client report without anyone retyping it, and every step should carry where it came from. Two links in that chain do not exist.

The break at the front costs accuracy

With no Page and no Fact, an assertion's provenance is somebody's memory of the site. That is not a hypothetical: “FDA Registered Facility” is live in a paid ad today and does not appear in the site's approved copy. It is probably true. Nothing in the chain can say so, and a Google reviewer checking a superlative or a certification claim lands on a page that does not carry it.

The break at the back costs money

With no Conversion object, bidding optimises toward whoever fills in a form. For a contract manufacturer that is mostly the wrong buyer — and the CRM already holds twelve pipeline stages that know which ones were right. Every week without the outbox is a week of signal the account will never get, because the 90-day match window closes on those clicks whether or not we ever upload them.

The middle of the chain — Claim, Lead, Period — is the part that already exists, and all three are gated on a human by design. That is deliberate and stays: you promote a claim, you release a report. The machine prepares, you publish. Nothing in this document changes that.

Six things a bank would have caught on day one

live exposure

“FDA Registered Facility” is in an ad and not on the site

The newest instance, and the strongest argument in this section, because it happened after the proposal that predicted it. The phrase is live in the approved RSA set. It appears nowhere in the site's approved copy. “FDA Approved” is a permanent disapproval; “FDA Registered” is accurate and survives review — but only with the registration visible on the page the ad points at.

So the claim needs a primary document and a page, or the headline needs to come out. This is precisely the class of error an extractor catches for free and a human writing headlines from memory cannot.

resolved

The brand row was a tense problem, and the homepage already fixed it

The pending row asserts a third-party manufacturer that doesn't sell its own consumer brand. As written it is unsourced — that idea appears on 0 of 63 pages and “Zemvelo” appears zero times. The only nearby sentence, /our-story's “Starting from our own brand…”, describes the origin. Both sentences are true at different times.

The approved present-tense form turns out to be the homepage h1 already:

“Award-winning supplement manufacturing for established brands”

The finding worth keeping is the mechanism: an extractor matching on topic rather than on the exact sentence would have promoted the /our-story line and sourced the opposite of what the row means. Verbatim text and a first-seen date are what stop that.

absent

“2,500 units” appears nowhere on the site

The positioning doc gives toll filling a 2,500-unit small run. Zero occurrences across 45,386 words. What the site publishes instead, on /services/toll-filling, is a flat MOQ 5,000 units — and /services/custom-manufacturing shows “SMALL RUN (ADDITIONAL FEE IF ACCEPTED) 5,000 units”.

The internal doc and the public site disagree about the cheapest way into a toll project. That is a sales-facing discrepancy, and it is exactly the class of thing that gets into an ad headline and then into a contract argument.

vocabulary conflict

The possessive rule is wrong, not the site

CLIENT.md is emphatic: always NSF's Certified for Sport®, with the possessive, and never the other form in copy that asserts the certification.

“NSF's Certified for Sport”0 pages
“NSF Certified for Sport”63 pages

The live site uses the non-possessive form universally, including in the site-wide meta description. Under the ruling that the site is approved copy, the pack's VOCABULARY slot is the thing that needs correcting.

precision gap

The site is more specific than the claim

The pending row says “NSF cGMP certified”. The site names the actual standard on 19 pages. /why-choose-mineralife:

“Mineralife operates under NSF 455-2 cGMP Certified standard.”

A bank sourced from pages inherits that precision for free. A bank sourced from a positioning doc rounds it off — and the rounded version is the weaker line to take into a market where the buyer knows what 455-2 is.

conflict

Two pages name the award, and they name it differently

The homepage says “award-winning” and never says which award. Three other pages do, and two of them give it a different title: a 2022 blog post says “Top 10 Contract Manufacturer”; /certifications says “Top 10 Contract Packaging Services Companies”. Contract manufacturing and contract packaging are not the same category, and the second is the weaker of the two for a manufacturer.

Both are client-approved copy, so both are assertable — which is the situation a bank needs a conflict state for. Your call was ads use Top 10 Contract Manufacturer and /certifications stays as written, which makes the conflict permanent: a crawler that re-raises it every run is noise, and one that quietly drops the losing sentence is lying about what the site says. The resolution is a third object — a dated decision recorded against the pair, keeping both facts, marking one preferred and the other superseded.

Chrome is not a fact

The naive version — regex the page text, keep the sentences mentioning a spec — produces something worse than nothing. On the real crawl it returned 417 raw matches, 279 unique, and counted the site-wide meta description 34 times as 34 separate findings. The first “sentence” of /blog/mineralife-is-hiring came back as the nav, the page title, a 2022 date and a real 21 CFR reference glued together, because the tags between them were stripped without inserting a boundary. It matches on cgmp and would enter the bank as a compliance fact.

Four rules that make the output usable

  • Scope to main content. Strip header, nav, footer and the consent banner before extracting. The site is Next.js with consistent landmarks, so this is a selector list, not a heuristic.
  • Dedupe by normalised text, keep every location. One fact, N pages. A sentence on more than about a third of the site is chrome, and is recorded as chrome rather than as a finding.
  • Attribute to the most specific page. A spec appearing on /faq and on twelve /serving pages belongs to /faq; the rest are echoes.
  • Never join across a tag boundary. Insert a separator when flattening, so a heading and the paragraph under it stay two things.

Key on the entity, not on the claim's own vocabulary

Checking whether the site substantiates “award-winning” by searching for award returns exactly 1 page — the homepage, which is the page making the claim. Searching for the entity instead — Manufacturing Outlook, Top 10 — returns 3 pages, including a dedicated post. Same corpus, same question, opposite answer. The page that proves a claim is rarely the page that phrases it the way the claim does.

And weight by fact density, not length. /faq is 414 words — shorter than every /serving page and most of the blog — and carries published MOQs for all three divisions, the full capsule size list, the bottle count range, the fill range, and all four scope limits in one sentence. Weighting by word count would rank it near the bottom.

Every candidate needs a compliance pass, not a crop

COMPLIANCE line 2 in CLIENT.md is a hard line: never name or imply a customer brand, and no photos of labeled finished goods that identify one. 04 — movement two

The campaign exists. The objects behind it do not.

This movement has changed the most. When it was written there was no Ads account, no landing page with a form, and no evidence a click could carry its id to a Lead row. All three are now done, by hand, and they work. All of it went to DataForSEO's Google Ads endpoint — 178 queryable, 5 rejected for exceeding Google's ten-word limit — and the answer came back for $0.09.

Every regional keyword is zero: colorado supplement manufacturer, supplement manufacturer colorado springs, supplement manufacturer denver, contract manufacturer colorado — all of them, 0/mo. The demand is in the vocabulary the pack avoids

The one term with real volume is contract supplement manufacturer — 1,600/mo, $31.03 — which the keyword sheet annotates “use sparingly” and the positioning doc treats as off-terminology. Underneath it: nutraceutical contract manufacturer 720 at $7.95, custom supplement manufacturer 320 at $38.28.

The approved identity — an award-winning contract manufacturer for established brands — is the same phrase as the only paid demand on the board. Convenient, and the live account is built on it.

Two of the head terms are the same term

supplement contract manufacturer and contract supplement manufacturer both return 1,600/mo at $31.03 with competition index 7 — identical on every field, because Google normalises word order and reports one number twice. Summed naively the commercial pool reads 5,580/mo; deduplicated it is 3,980. Any budget model built on the raw sum starts 40% optimistic, and a spreadsheet of estimates cannot catch that. Google reviews assertions separately, and truth is not the test

The good news first: Google's healthcare and medicines policy does not prohibit this business model. It regulates promotion of supplements to consumers; a B2B manufacturing service is not that, and no LegitScript-style certification applies. The scanners still read the landing page, so ad copy and pages must stay off consumer health outcomes — which Mineralife's copy already does.

Three risks that have nothing to do with whether a statement is true:

  • Superlatives are enforced under the misrepresentation policy, and what passes is landing-page evidence carrying the awarding body, the category, and a visible date inside roughly 12–24 months. The Manufacturing Outlook listing is 2022, so publishing it with the year makes the disapproval more likely, not less. This is why “Award-Winning Manufacturer” is out of the live headline set, and it should stay out unless a 2025 or 2026 recognition exists.
  • Certification marks — NSF Certified for Sport® and NSF 455-2 cGMP — flag under the trademark policy unless authorisation is on file with Google or the use is strictly descriptive. Descriptive use is arguable here and worth arguing, but not inside the account's only campaign, which is why they belong in an ad group of their own.
  • Registration claims — “FDA Approved” is a permanent disapproval; “FDA Registered” is accurate and survives, but only with the registration visible on the page it points at. It is live today without one. Never let this one drift in an edit.

The structural answer is unchanged: flagged assertions launch in their own ad group, so a disapproval takes down one group rather than the account.

What works, and better than most implementations

  • AttributionCapture.tsx writes on mount and on every client navigation, so a click landing on / and converting on /contact keeps its gclid.
  • attribution.ts stores first- and last-touch and handles gbraid/wbraid — the consent-mode variants most implementations drop, which is where iOS and consent-limited clicks go.
  • LeadForm.tsx and LeadIntakeWizard.tsx both call getAttributionForSubmit() and spread it into the POST body; /api/leads runs it through pickAttributionFields. Prisma has gclid, gbraid, wbraid with @@index([gclid]), same on CatalogDownload.
  • It is independent of the consent gate, because it is localStorage rather than GTM — so a gclid survives a visitor who never accepts GTM-5B83HMG. That matters much more than it looks now that the cookieless counter says GA4 sees roughly 8% of visitors.
  • And it is observed, which it was not when this was drafted: August ran form_submit 9 : generate_lead 9, with the click id stored server-side at submission.

What is missing

  • No upload path. The CRM can now read the Ads account — lib/insights/google.ts carries a paged GAQL client — but uploadClickConversions is zero matches in every repo.
  • No conversion value. Every lead is worth the same to Google, so it optimises toward whoever fills in a form, which for a manufacturer is mostly the wrong buyer.
  • No hashed first-party data. Google's current guidance is Enhanced Conversions for Leads — the click id plus hashed email and phone in the same payload, which recovers cross-device journeys and clicks whose id was cleared. The Lead model already holds both fields; it is a hashing step, not a schema change.
  • No per-kind expiry. A stored gbraid older than a fortnight is a row the uploader will always be rejected on, and today every click id is given 90 days.

One implementation note: direct offline-conversion uploads migrated toward the Data Manager API framework during 2026, so the integration target should be confirmed against current docs on the day rather than copied from an older example.

The sales cycle is longer than Google's memory

Offline conversion import matches on a click id for 90 days; past that the upload is rejected outright. Contract manufacturing runs a 60–180 day cycle. Spread evenly across that range, only about a quarter of won deals would land inside the window; the other three quarters happen and Google never hears about them. (Illustrative — it assumes an even spread, which nobody has measured for this client. The real distribution is something the CRM will know after a year.)

Optimising for leads sidesteps most of this for now, which is why the deeper stages are secondary: recorded, not bid on. The window problem arrives in full the day you switch to bidding on quotes or POs, which is exactly why they get recorded from the start. expired stays a first-class state rather than an error — counting the wins that land too late is how you learn whether the channel works better than Google can see.

// proposed — the same tick, asking a table what is due
for (const job of dueJobs(now)) {        // scheduled_jobs: id, interval, last_run_at
  await report(job, "running", runId)          // /api/events/run — already exists
  try {
    const stats = await job.run()

Every job reports through /api/events/run, which already exists and already opens or escalates a ticket on failure and stands it down on success — so no new alerting is needed. Four of the five reporting jobs are pure CRM work and belong on tallkarol-cron without argument. The fifth is the Search Console pull into daedalus-insights/data/, and that CLI runs on your laptop against a local git repo. Railway cron on the CRM cannot call it.

Why Page and Fact are two objects and not one

A Page asserts nothing, which is what makes it admissible under the rule that live copy cannot source a claim. It is an observation with a date. A Fact is a sentence lifted out of one, still verbatim, and it is the thing that can be tested against a pending claim row. Collapsing them would mean the crawler's own judgement is baked into the evidence, and the diff between two crawls stops being meaningful.

Why Assertion is not just a shorter Claim

Because compression is where truth gets lost, and it needs to be reviewable separately. “Toll projects publish a 5,000-unit MOQ” becomes “Toll MOQ: 5,000 units” and the figure survives; it could as easily have become “Low MOQs”, which is a different kind of statement with no fact behind it. Recording the fact id and what was dropped is what lets someone check the second kind never happened.

Settled, and still open

Settled — recorded so none of it gets relitigated

decided

You are the promotion gate, everywhere

A fact never becomes a claim on its own, an assertion never goes live without approval, and a report is filed rather than sent. The machine prepares, you publish. This is the one rule the whole document is arranged around and nothing in it proposes an exception.

decided

The site is approved copy

All three corpora — inherited, templated, authored — are assertable, with origin recorded per fact so a 2022 hiring post is never mistaken for the /faq spec table. Certifications remain gated:document: the site can source how Mineralife describes itself, not that a certification is held.

Do not bid on private label supplement manufacturer

This reverses my earlier recommendation, and the reversal is right. The counter-argument won: removing it takes the white-label ad group below the volume where its landing page earns its keep, and the terminology discipline is worth more than the reach.

Open — seven decisions and two confirmations

1. What happens to the archive on your laptop? blocks R-12

What I need: a choice between three, none of them wrong.

A — the CRM absorbs the pull. Port Search Console archiving into tallkarol/crm and write windows to Postgres instead of files. One system, one clock, one backup. Costs a port, and daedalus-insights becomes an analysis tool over data it no longer owns.

B — insights gets deployed too. A second Railway service running the CLI on its own cron, keeping the file archive as a committed repo. Truest to the existing split, and it is a second thing to deploy and watch.

C — leave it manual, honestly. You run insights pull when you think of it. Cheapest today, and it is the failure mode this movement is about.

My call: A. You have decided the clock lives in one spot, and an archive the clock cannot reach is not really scheduled. The pull is one API call and a write — far less code than keeping two deployments in step — and the eighteen windows already collected import once and never need moving again.

2. Can “FDA Registered Facility” be substantiated? live exposure

What I need: the FDA facility registration on file, and a decision to publish it on the pages the ads point at — or instruction to pull the headline.

Why I'm asking: it is running in a paid ad now and appears on 0 of 63 pages. The claim is very likely true; that is not the test. The test is whether a reviewer checking it lands on a page that carries it, and today they do not.

My call: get the registration, publish it in the trust bar with the registration number visible, and leave the headline in. If the document is not readily to hand, pull the headline this week and put it back when it is. Do not run it uncited for another month.
