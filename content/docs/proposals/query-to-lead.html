03 — the shift

Three objects, one of them new

Part one ends with a Claim: a verbatim sentence with a page behind it. An ad cannot hold a verbatim sentence — a headline is thirty characters. So there has to be something between a claim and a headline, and it has to be reviewable, or the whole citation chain breaks at exactly the point where the assertion becomes public.

exists — gains a price

A Keyword

Already in the sheet with an estimate. Gains live volume, a CPC, a competition index and a fetch date, so a term that dies stays visible as a term that died rather than disappearing from the plan. Priced the same way facts are dated: from a run, not from judgement.

data/mineralife/keywords.json

new

An Assertion

One ad-ready string — a 30-character headline, a 90-character description — carrying the ID of the fact it compresses and the compression itself as a reviewable act. “NSF 455-2 cGMP Certified” is not a quote of anything on the site. It is MIN-C-01 squeezed into 24 characters, and somebody has to have agreed that the squeeze is still true.

data/mineralife/assertions.json

new

A Conversion

Not a form submit. A Lead crossing a pipeline stage, with a value attached and a gclid to send it back on. A form fill and a PO are both “conversions” to a tag manager; they are three orders of magnitude apart to the business, and Google bids on whichever number you hand it.

mineralife-crm — Lead.stage → offline import

Why an Assertion is not just a shorter Claim

Because compression is where a true sentence becomes a false one, and it happens silently. “Toll projects publish a 5,000-unit MOQ” is 39 characters and sourced. “Low MOQ toll filling” is 20 characters, fits, sounds better, and is not what the site says — 5,000 units is not a low MOQ in this market, and the claim bank has no row that says it is. Nothing in a claims table catches that, because the claim it descends from is still perfectly true.

So the assertion records both strings and the fact ID, an approval date, and which campaign it is live in. When a fact goes stale in part one's decay check, every assertion built on it is flagged in the same pass — which is the only reason a page-sourced ad is safe to run for a year.

Google reviews this separately, and the award is the casualty

The good news first: Google's healthcare and medicines policy does not prohibit this business model. It regulates promotion of supplements to consumers; a B2B manufacturing service isn't that, and no LegitScript-style certification applies. The scanners still read the landing page, so ad copy and pages must stay off consumer health outcomes — which Mineralife's copy already does.

Three of the twelve headlines carry risk that has nothing to do with whether they are true.

  • Award-Winning Manufacturer — superlatives are enforced under the misrepresentation policy, and what passes review is landing-page evidence carrying the awarding body, the category, and a visible date, typically inside 12–24 months. The Manufacturing Outlook listing is 2022. Four years old is a legacy claim, and a legacy date is specifically what triggers the automated disapproval — so putting the award on the page, which was my recommendation yesterday, does not rescue this headline. It is the wrong fix. See section 09.
  • NSF Certified for Sport® and NSF 455-2 cGMP — registered certification marks in a headline flag under the trademark policy unless authorisation is on file with Google or the use is strictly descriptive. Descriptive use is arguable here and worth arguing, but not worth arguing inside the account's only campaign.
  • FDA Registered — not in my twelve, and it is in the homepage trust bar, which is where the reviewer looks. “FDA Approved” is a permanent disapproval; “FDA Registered” is accurate and survives, but only with the registration visible on the page it points at. Never let this one drift in an edit.

The structural answer is the same: the flagged headlines launch in their own ad group, so a disapproval takes down one group rather than the account.

06 — the conversion loop

Three quarters built, and the last quarter is the one that matters

The click-to-CRM half of the loop is already working in code, and better than most implementations. What is missing is everything after the Lead row.

4capture points wired gclid, gbraid, wbraid and UTMs, first- and last-touch.
90dfirst-touch TTL Right for gclid. 6.4× too generous for gbraid/wbraid, which expire at 14.
12pipeline stages New through Won — PO Received. The value ladder already exists.
0conversions uploaded No Ads client in any repo. Google sees none of it.

What already works

  • AttributionCapture.tsx writes on mount and on every client navigation, so a click that lands on / and converts on /contact keeps its gclid.
  • attribution.ts stores first- and last-touch with a 90-day TTL, and handles gbraid/wbraid — the consent-mode variants most implementations drop, which is where iOS and consent-limited clicks go.
  • LeadForm.tsx:50 and LeadIntakeWizard.tsx:688 both call getAttributionForSubmit() and spread it into the POST body.
  • /api/leads runs it through pickAttributionFields and attributionToPrismaData; Prisma has gclid, gbraid, wbraid with @@index([gclid]). Same on CatalogDownload.

It is also independent of the consent gate, because it is localStorage rather than GTM — so a gclid survives a visitor who never accepts GTM-5B83HMG. Worth knowing deliberately rather than by accident.

What is missing

  • No Ads API client anywhere. Zero matches for uploadClickConversions or conversionAction across all three repos.
  • No conversion value. Every lead is worth the same to Google, so it optimises toward whoever fills in a form — which for a manufacturer is mostly the wrong buyer.
  • No AW- conversion id in the site's HTML, so even an online conversion has nowhere to land today.
  • Nobody has watched a real click carry a gclid from ad to Lead row on production. The code is right; the end-to-end has never been observed. That is a fifteen-minute test with a tagged URL, and it belongs before spend, not after.
  • No hashed first-party data in the upload. Google's current guidance is Enhanced Conversions for Leads — gclid plus hashed email and phone in the same payload, which recovers cross-device journeys and clicks whose id was cleared. The Lead model already holds both fields; it is a hashing step, not a schema change.

One implementation note for whoever builds it: direct offline-conversion uploads migrated toward the Data Manager API framework during 2026, so the integration target should be confirmed against current docs on the day rather than copied from an older example.

Four signals, no prices — optimise for leads, learn the rest

Twelve stages already exist in src/lib/crm/pipeline/defaults.ts. Your call is to optimise for leads in general and set target ROAS next year off real data, so none of these carry a dollar value yet. What they do carry is a primary or secondary designation: Google bids on the primary and merely records the rest. That is the whole difference, and it means the deeper stages accumulate a year of history in the account without steering anything until you decide they should.

StageConversion actionRoleWhy this one
newLead submittedprimarywhat Google bids toward. Fires immediately, so the account starts learning from week one
qualifiedQualified leadsecondaryfirst point a human agreed it is a real buyer — the ratio to become next year's target
quote_sentQuote sentsecondarystrongest mid-funnel signal that reliably lands inside 90 days
wonPO receivedsecondarythe revenue truth, recorded from day one so it is there when you want to bid on it

discovery_call, formulation_spec, sample_requested and negotiation stay internal — more upload traffic, no extra signal. lost matters too, but only once there is enough volume for it to mean anything. Bidding starts on Maximize Conversions with no target: a target CPA set before there is data to set it from is a guess that throttles delivery, which is the opposite of getting traffic moving.

The sales cycle is longer than Google's memory, and now we can size it

Offline conversion import matches on gclid for 90 days from the click; past that the upload is rejected outright. Contract manufacturing runs a 60–180 day cycle — custom formulation, stability, contract terms. Spread evenly across that range, only about a quarter of won deals would land inside the window; the other three quarters happen, and Google never hears about them. (Illustrative — it assumes an even spread across the cited range, which nobody has measured for this client. The real distribution is something the CRM will know after a year.)

Optimising for leads sidesteps most of this for now. A lead submission happens at the click, so it is never near the window; only the deeper stages are exposed, and those are secondary — recorded, not bid on. The window problem arrives in full the day you switch to bidding on quotes or POs, which is the point of recording them from the start. Until then quote_sent is the signal to watch, and expired stays a first-class state in the schema below rather than an error: counting the wins that land too late is how you learn whether the channel works better than Google can see.

gbraid and wbraid are 14 days, not 90 — and attribution.ts gives everything the same 90-day TTL. Nothing breaks, but a stored gbraid older than a fortnight is a row the uploader will always be rejected on. The outbox should carry the window per click-id kind and expire those early rather than posting them and reading the error.

-- Conversion uploads are an outbox, not a column on Lead: one row per
-- (lead, action) so a re-run is idempotent and a rejection is inspectable.
CREATE TABLE conversion_upload (
  id            uuid PRIMARY KEY DEFAULT gen_random_uuid(),
  lead_id       uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES "Lead"(id) ON DELETE CASCADE,
  action        text NOT NULL,        -- lead_submitted | qualified | quote_sent | po_received
  click_id      text NOT NULL,        -- gclid, gbraid or wbraid, whichever the lead carries
  click_id_kind text NOT NULL,        -- gclid = 90d window; gbraid/wbraid = 14d
  expires_at    timestamptz NOT NULL,  -- click time + the window for THIS kind, not a flat 90
  email_sha256  text,                -- enhanced conversions for leads: sent alongside the
  phone_sha256  text,                -- click id, recovers cross-device and cleared ids
  value_usd     numeric(10,2),       -- left null in 2026; the column exists so the
                                     -- tROAS switch next year is a backfill, not a migration
  occurred_at   timestamptz NOT NULL,  -- when the STAGE changed, not when we uploaded
  uploaded_at   timestamptz,
  status        text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'pending',  -- pending | uploaded | rejected | expired
  error         text,
  UNIQUE (lead_id, action)
);
CREATE INDEX conversion_upload_pending ON conversion_upload (status, occurred_at)
  WHERE status = 'pending';

expired is a real state, not an error state: it records a deal that closed after Google's window shut. Counting those is how you find out whether the sales cycle is outrunning the attribution, which is a fact about the business worth knowing on its own.

08 — sequence

Build order

Five increments. The first one is a form to fill in, and it should happen today, because everything after increment three waits on it.

1

Generate the token, apply for Basic

The token issues immediately from the API Center in TALLKAROL's manager account — no client action needed, the agency token works over a linked sub-account. Explorer access arrives with it but cannot create accounts, so apply for Basic the same day: two business days is the target, longer when Google is backlogged. It gates increments 4 and 5 and nothing else.

Ships: the only third-party dependency starts clearing on day one.

2

Prove one gclid end to end

Load a production URL with a test gclid, submit the real /contact form, read the Lead row. The code path is correct on inspection; nobody has watched it work. Fifteen minutes, before any money is spent.

Ships: the attribution assumption becomes an observation.

3

Assertions, approved and stored

Compress the eight facts into the twelve headlines and four descriptions, each carrying its fact ID and its compression note, and get them approved in one pass. Flag the superlative and the certification mark for their own ad group.

Ships: every string an ad can say, with a page behind it.

4

The conversion outbox — before the traffic, not after

The migration, the stage-change hook that writes a pending row, and the uploader that drains it, with Lead submitted as the primary action and the other three recorded as secondary. Feeding conversions back is the point of the whole exercise, so it should be working before the first click rather than chased afterwards.

Ships: the feedback loop exists before there is anything to feed into it.

Safety net if it does slip: the gclid is already persisted on every Lead row, so anything captured in the first 90 days can be backfilled. The data is not lost, only late — which is a reason not to panic, not a reason to reorder.

5

Campaigns written paused, then you enable them

Three campaigns, five ad groups, the negative list, budgets, and the RSAs built from increment 3 — created through the API in paused state. You review in the Ads UI and enable. Nothing here enables a campaign.

Ships: traffic starts flowing, into a loop that is already listening.

09 — before building

One thing left, and two now settled

Two of the four are answered: optimise for leads, and the budget is the budget. What they changed is recorded below the open one.

The only open question: do we buy “private label”?

What I need: yes or no on bidding a term Mineralife's copy refuses to use.

Why I'm asking: private label supplement manufacturer is 590/mo at $24.30 — nearly twice the volume of custom supplement manufacturer — and the sheet marks it DO NOT TARGET on terminology grounds. In organic that is correct. In paid, the keyword is what the buyer types; the ad and the page still answer in your words.

My call: buy it, phrase match, in its own ad group with its own budget so it can be judged on its own numbers and switched off cleanly. The risk is a buyer arriving with the wrong model in mind, which is a landing-page problem, and /services/white-label already exists to solve it. Your stated goal — traffic with intent, flowing now — points the same way: somebody typing “private label supplement manufacturer” is looking for exactly this business, using a word it prefers not to use about itself.

decided

Optimise for leads. No conversion values this year.

Google bids on Lead submitted; Qualified, Quote sent and PO received are uploaded as secondary actions, recorded but not steering anything. Bidding starts on Maximize Conversions with no target CPA, because a target set before there is data to set it from throttles delivery. The value_usd column exists and stays null, so switching to target ROAS next year is a backfill rather than a migration — and by then the account holds a year of qualified-to-quote-to-PO ratios to set the target from.

A useful side effect: optimising on a signal that fires at the click means the 90-day attribution window barely bites this year. It bites the day you switch to bidding on quotes, which is exactly why they get recorded from day one.

decided

The budget is the budget. Get traffic moving.

So the arithmetic in section 09's earlier draft stops being a decision and becomes an expectation-setter: at $25–31 a click, a couple of thousand a month is a couple of thousand clicks a year, single-digit leads a month, and a sample too thin to optimise on for a while. None of that is a reason to wait — it is the reason the first month runs narrow, on the core campaign only, exact and phrase, no broad. The listening post gets switched on once the conversion loop is confirmed to be reporting.

One thing worth spending on ahead of clicks: paid traffic should land on a page that has the form on it. All five competitors do this and the homepage does not. At these prices it is the cheapest conversion-rate improvement available.

Standing recommendation: drop “award-winning” unless something newer exists

What I need: either a recognition from the last year or two, or a decision to stop saying “award-winning” in ads.

Why I'm asking: yesterday I said put the award on the landing page and the headline would run. That was wrong, and I'm correcting it. What passes a superlative review is landing-page evidence with a visible date, and the reviewer expects that date to be recent — roughly the last 12 to 24 months. Manufacturing Outlook named Mineralife in 2022. Publishing it with the year makes the claim honest and makes the disapproval more likely, not less, because a legacy date is exactly the pattern the automated check looks for. Publishing it without the year is the thing I would not do.

My call: drop “Award-Winning Manufacturer” from the headline set. It costs one headline of twelve and removes the single most likely cause of a disapproval on a brand-new account, which is the worst moment to have one. The award stays on the site and stays in the fact bank — it is true, and it is good sales material in a call. It is just not an ad headline in 2026. If a 2025 or 2026 recognition exists that I haven't found, this reverses immediately and the headline goes back in.

Where the benchmark numbers come from

The conversion rates, CPLs, close rates and cycle lengths in this section are published category benchmarks for B2B industrial and manufacturing search, gathered in a separate research pass on 30 August 2026 — not measurements of this client, who has never run a paid campaign. They are load-bearing for the budget question and for the 90-day window argument, so they should be replaced with Mineralife's own numbers as soon as there are any. Everything else in this proposal is measured: the keyword figures are a live DataForSEO pull, and the code citations were read from the repos.
